Aspen occupies a unique position in the luxury rental market. It is year-round — ski season, summer festivals, the Ideas Festival, the Food and Wine Classic, shoulder season retreats — with estates that command rates well above comparable mountain markets. During peak season, nightly rates for top-tier properties range from $10,000 to $50,000, with pricing driven primarily by bedroom count and proximity to ski slopes and exclusive neighborhoods. The guests are high-net-worth individuals who travel with intent and return to properties they trust.

For a property operating at the top tier of the Aspen market, Airbnb’s current 15.5% host fee represents a significant and entirely controllable cost — not a cost of doing business, but a cost of using the wrong channel. On $1M in annual bookings, that figure is $155,000. On $2M, it is $310,000. These are not hypothetical numbers—they’re Airbnb’s current published host-only fee, in effect since October 2025.

What makes Aspen particularly well-suited to direct booking is the guest profile. Aspen guests plan deliberately — not impulse bookings. They know where they want to stay, they’ve stayed before, and they are already thinking about next year. A private direct channel captures that second booking, and the one after that, without a platform in the middle.

The same model that Aman, Four Seasons, and Auberge use at scale — own the guest relationship, communicate directly, earn repeat business through the channel you control — is available to individual property owners. Loftare brings it to the property level, built for one property, with no per-booking commission and no third-party SaaS license—just a light monthly maintenance fee to keep the platform running.
